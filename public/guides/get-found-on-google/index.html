How Texas detailers show up
for "detailing near me."

Roughly 133,000 people a month in the US type "car detailing near me." Google answers with three shops in a map, then a handful of websites. This is the checklist for being one of them in your town — whether you're mobile or run a bay. No agency-speak.

1. Your Google Business Profile does most of the work

The map with three shops in it is called the local pack, and it's decided almost entirely by your Google Business Profile — not your website. Most detailers have one that's half-finished. Finish it.

  • Primary category: "Car detailing service." Not "Car wash," not "Auto repair shop." Add secondaries only for what you actually sell (window tinting, ceramic coating shows up under detailing).
  • Mobile? Set it up as a service-area business. Hide your street address and list every city and zip you actually drive to. Google will show you across that whole area instead of a two-mile circle around your house. This single setting is why some mobile detailers rank in eight towns and others in none.
  • Add every package as a Service with a price. "Full Interior Detail — from $149." Google reads these and matches them to searches like "interior car detailing."
  • Hours, phone, website link, booking link. The booking link goes straight to your site's booking section, not your Instagram.
  • Business description: two plain sentences naming your services and your cities. "Mobile detailing in Deer Park, Pasadena, La Porte and Baytown. Interior details, paint correction and ceramic coatings at your driveway or office."

2. You need a real website — Instagram doesn't count

Google can't rank your Instagram posts for "car detailing near me." Your profile links somewhere; if that's a Facebook page or a Linktree, you've handed the click to a competitor who has prices and a Book button. The site needs to do three things in five seconds on a phone: show your work, show your prices, let them book.

Test: pull up your own site on your phone, on cellular, right now. Can you see a price and tap Book without scrolling? If not, that's the job.

3. Show your prices. Yes, really.

"Car detailing prices" and "car detailing cost" are among the most-searched phrases in the whole industry, and Google increasingly answers them directly from pages that publish numbers. "Text for a quote" gets you filtered out — by Google and by the customer who's comparing three tabs at 9pm.

You don't need one price for every vehicle. Small / SUV / Truck & XL columns, or "from $149," is enough. It kills the "what do you charge?" DM and pre-qualifies the tire-kickers.

4. Photos: before/after, in your town, on your site and your profile

Detailing sells a visible transformation. A gallery page beats any paragraph you could write. Rules:

  • Real photos of real cars you did. No stock, no renders.
  • Before and after side by side. Seats, wheel wells, headlights, swirl marks in the sun.
  • Name the file and the caption: interior-detail-f150-deer-park.jpg, "Full interior, F‑150, Deer Park." That text is what Google indexes.
  • Upload the same photos to your Business Profile weekly. Profiles with fresh photos get more clicks and more calls.

5. Reviews: count, recency, and the words in them

You need enough reviews to pass the third shop in your town's map pack, then a steady trickle. Recency matters as much as count — twenty reviews from 2023 lose to eight from this quarter.

  • Ask at hand-off, in person, when they're looking at the car. Then text the direct review link before you leave the driveway.
  • Ask them to say what and where. "Ceramic coating on my Tacoma in Pasadena" in a review is a ranking signal you can't buy.
  • Reply to every one in a sentence. Google notices, and so does the next customer reading them.
  • Pull the best three onto your site's homepage with the customer's first name and town.

6. One page per service, one page per city

Here's where a five-page site beats a one-page site. Google matches searches to pages, not to businesses. If the only page you have is a homepage that says "detailing," you're competing for one phrase. Split it up:

PageWins searches like
/interior-detailing/interior car detailing, car seat shampoo near me
/ceramic-coating/ceramic coating near me, ceramic coating cost
/paint-correction/paint correction near me, swirl mark removal
/mobile-detailing-pasadena-tx/mobile detailing Pasadena, car detailing Pasadena TX
/mobile-detailing-baytown-tx/mobile detailing Baytown, detailer near me (from Baytown)

Each page: a heading with the service and city, 150–300 honest words about how you do it there, prices, two or three photos from that town, and the Book button. Not spun copy — Google penalizes twenty pages that say the same thing with the city name swapped. Write what's different: the HEB parking lot you meet people at, the water situation in older neighborhoods, whatever's true.

Ceramic coating deserves special attention. It's the highest ticket on your menu and, in most Texas metros, barely contested organically. A proper page — what coating you use, prep steps, warranty, price by vehicle size, before/after — can outrank chains within weeks.

7. The technical stuff (short)

  • Speed. Under two seconds on a phone. Compress photos. Skip page builders that load 2 MB of scripts before your hero image.
  • Title tags that say the thing. "Mobile Detailing in Pasadena, TX | Bayou Shine Detail" — not "Home."
  • Same name, address, phone everywhere. Site, Google, Facebook, Yelp, Apple Maps. "Bayou Shine Detail" and "Bayou Shine Detailing LLC" look like two businesses to a machine.
  • Schema markup. Hidden code that tells Google "this is a LocalBusiness; here are its services, hours, area and reviews." Every site we build ships with it.
  • Click-to-call and a Book button in the top bar on mobile. Always visible.
  • HTTPS, a real domain (yourshop.com, not a builder subdomain), and a sitemap submitted in Google Search Console.

The checklist

  1. Business Profile: correct category, service-area setup if mobile, every package listed with a price, description naming your cities.
  2. A site on your own domain that shows work, prices and a Book button above the fold on a phone.
  3. Prices published, at least "from $X" by vehicle size.
  4. Before/after gallery with descriptive captions; same photos on your profile weekly.
  5. Review ask at every hand-off, direct link texted, replies to all.
  6. A page per service and per city you actually serve, with real differences between them.
  7. Fast, consistent NAP, schema, click-to-call, HTTPS, Search Console.
